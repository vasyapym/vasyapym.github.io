  (quorum overprint / copper trace) stopped ("these are not these").
- Round 5 (ten concepts, ten graphic languages): owner picked candidate I

  seven keyholes-as-nodes on a ring, four keys turned (solid, seated) linked

  New direction supplied by a reference image (described below):
  **cybernetics**. Owner's words: "thematically i like this one but it is not
  appropriate as it is for project card illustration, of course. work on this
  direction. give 10 variants."

This round the owner wants YOU to brainstorm: ten different cybernetic
concepts, each implemented as a complete mark.

## THE REFERENCE IMAGE (you cannot see it — this is the full description)

File: reference-images/cybernetics.jpg — a black poster, portrait format,
t-shirt-graphic style, white ink only.

- One central **CPU/chip package**: a white square (about 1/5 of the poster's
  width) with a dashed/stitched inner margin like a solder frame and a blank
  white face, sitting upper-centre-right of the composition.
- From all four sides of the chip, **white circuit traces** fan outward —
  routed at strict 90° and 45° elbows, branching like roots or dendrites,
  denser near the chip, thinning as they escape toward the corners. A few
  long traces make daring solo runs to far corners.
- Every trace ends in one of a rich **terminal vocabulary**: solid solder
  dots, open circles (rings), small squares, rounded pads, dashed tick marks,
  dotted clusters, tiny resistor packages (small rectangles with pin stubs),
  small IC packages, short parallel dash groups.
- The composition is **asymmetric and organic**: huge negative space, density
  clustered around the chip, the whole reading as a living machine — a
  processor growing a nervous system.

What to take from it: the cybernetic THEME (self-regulating machine,
circuit-as-organism, control and communication), the terminal hardware
vocabulary, the branching/escaping trace grammar, the asymmetric composition
with one dominant mass. What NOT to take: the literal portrait CPU-poster
layout, the white-on-black monochrome (the family technique below overrides
it), and pin-perfect symmetric chip fan-outs as a centrepiece.

## What every variant must still say (Raft consensus, read in under a second)

The mark illustrates the Raft Cluster project card. At card size (~260×160,
also seen at 240px wide on mobile) a viewer must read, without text:

1. A small set of NODES (3–5 identifiable participants); the leader
   identifiable WITHOUT text via a deep-coral crown ring + coral presence;
   followers neutral.
2. Committed vs unwritten as two visibly different textures (solid/overprinted
   vs dashed/empty). Where the concept shows commit progress, a committed
   majority must be legible.
3. One live coral PROTAGONIST in flight (entry / vote / signal) — the "alive"
   element.
4. The laggard's deficiency (a broken trace, a dashed pad, a missed arrival,
   a dead end).
5. A visible direction of flow / rotation.

The reference's many small terminals (pads, ticks, resistor packages) may act
as committed log entries / signal hardware texture, but must not dilute the
node read — the 3–5 nodes stay the biggest, most structured elements after
the leader's mass.

## The family TECHNIQUE — keep all of it

Dark editorial ink catalogue, plate `#0b1317` showing through (**never paint
a background rect** — the card stage provides it); neutral ink ramp
`#26333b #465059 #7d7669 #b6ac95`; paper `#eeeae0 #f4efe4` sparingly; ONE
spot colour CORAL `#ff6a5f` (+ deep `#7d2723`); halftone dot screens; no
text, no numbers, no gradients, no filters; crisp primitives; poster
boldness — smallest element ≥ 2.5px (dashed "empty" accents may go to 1.5),
strokes 2.5–6 wide, ~30–50 primitives per mark (the owner asked for MORE
visual interest — use the rich end); coral ≤ 3 element groups per mark
(crown + core counts as one).

## Hard rules (all ten)

- SVG `viewBox="0 0 260 160"`, `aria-hidden="true"`, NO width/height
  attributes.
- Nothing outside x 16→244 / y 16→144 except the backdrop ellipse and halo.
- Patterns in `<defs>`, `patternUnits="userSpaceOnUse"`, ids prefixed
  `gem-raft7-<letter>-*` (e.g. `gem-raft7-a-dense`) — `gem-raft-*`,
  `gem-raft5-*`, `gem-raft6-*` are already live in the same rendered DOM.
- Standard screens per candidate: `dense` (7×7, dot r 1.9, `#ff6a5f`),
  `sparse` (11×11, dot r 1.6, `#7d7669`), `halo` (7×7, dot r 1.9, `#ff6a5f`)
  — plus any extra pattern the concept needs.
- Halo contract: exactly ONE ellipse `className="gem-halo"`
  `style={haloVar(0.12)}` `opacity={0.12}`, coral dots, over the focal
  mechanism. Wide sparse backdrop ellipse (~rx 104 ry 64, cx ~130 cy ~84,
  opacity 0.09). `haloVar` exists in module scope.
- Component: `export function RaftCyberA() { … }` … `RaftCyberJ` — one
  complete compilable TSX block per candidate, no placeholders, no imports
  needed.
- State the node arithmetic in your prose (e.g. "3 of 5 branches committed =
  majority").
- Squint test: each mark first reads as its cybernetic mechanism, second as
  Raft consensus; if it reads as generic electronics clip-art, rework it.
