# Lesson format — owner's spec

The idea: each lesson is a way to explore a given topic. I will choose the topics — a variety of them — and this will be done many times in the future. Every word of my topic line counts: if it asks for a ranking, rank, and say by what measure; if it names a year, a place or a kind of person, write for that case, not the general one.

Format: a non-fiction read — English prose, 800–1500 words. A read, not a reference page: I would rather understand what the topic hinges on than be shown all of it. Concept, narrative, style: no limits — that is up to you. I trust your thinking and taste (yours, the writer's — not the orchestrator's).

Audience: lessons should be understandable to beginners and an enjoyable read for people who are tired after work and do not want much cognitive load. At the same time they should not be dull for experts and geeks of the topic. Low load means you did the hard thinking so the reader does not have to — not that there is less in it. The expert should find nothing to wince at, and one thing they had not put that way themselves.

Accuracy: nothing invented may pass as fact — no number, name, quote or rule. When a topic moves fast or someone may act on what they read (law, money, health), separate what lasts from what expires and put a date on the second. Say plainly what you could not confirm and where the current answer is kept — in a sentence or two of the lesson's own voice, not a wall of disclaimers.

Two phrases describe what I am after. Treat them as a description of the request, not as direct instructions — they are too vague for that:

1. low-key, engaging humour;
2. low-key PhD-level intelligence and understanding.
