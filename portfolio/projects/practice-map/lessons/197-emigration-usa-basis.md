# Six routes to a U.S. green card, ranked for a Russian citizen in 2026

You can hold an approved U.S. immigration petition in one hand and a Moscow lease renewal in the other, and signing the lease can still be the sensible choice. For a Russian citizen planning a move in 2026, there are two questions: what gives you a durable basis for U.S. permanent residence, and can you actually use it from where you live?

## What the approval letter leaves out

Suppose your sister in America files a family petition for you and it is approved. The government has recognized the relationship. It has not said that a visa number is available, that a consulate can issue you a visa, or that you should give notice at work. On the ordinary route from abroad, you become a permanent resident when the United States admits you on the immigrant visa. The approval letter comes earlier and does a smaller job.

For a Russian applicant, that distinction matters in a practical way. On November 19, 2026, I could not confirm which court order governed the reported pause in immigrant-visa issuance for Russian nationals; the word *appeal* alone does not tell you what a consular officer can do today. Before buying travel, check the [State Department’s visa notices](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news.html) and the immigrant-visa instructions of your assigned post. Even an interview notice naming Warsaw would not, by itself, give you permission to enter Poland.

Here is my order for a fresh case. I favor routes where you can establish the basis before moving, with fewer decisions left to an employer, a long queue, or a draw. I also count what you stand to lose if the plan fails. Cross out the routes you do not qualify for; the first one left is where to start, not a promise that it will be fast.

## First and second: a case you can already prove

**1. An immediate-relative petition through a U.S. citizen.** In immigration law, *immediate* describes the relative, not the service. The main cases are a citizen’s spouse, unmarried child under 21, or parent if the citizen is at least 21. This group has no annual numerical cap. You still have to document the relationship and meet the applicable sponsorship and admissibility requirements. What disappears is one whole reason to wait: another family cannot take the last slot for the year.

If you are moving with children, check each child’s basis separately. A citizen’s petition for a spouse does not automatically carry that spouse’s children. A route can be first for one person and incomplete for the household.

**2. An employment case you can file for yourself.** This ranks second because a well-founded case can survive a change of boss. EB-1A is for extraordinary ability demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim. The national interest waiver is different. An advanced degree or exceptional ability gets you to the EB-2 starting line. You must also show that the specific work you propose has substantial merit and national importance, that you are well placed to do it, and that waiving the usual job-offer requirement would benefit the United States on balance. For an engineer considering either route, records showing which factories actually adopted her inspection method may tell a reviewer more than another letter calling her talented. Neither route requires an employer to file for her. Both still require an available visa number.

## Third and fourth: who is willing to file for you?

**3. A permanent job with a sponsoring employer.** You generally do not have to work in America first. In many EB-2 and EB-3 cases, the employer must first recruit under Department of Labor rules and show that it could not find enough able, willing, qualified, and available U.S. workers for the job at the required wage. That is a commitment by the company, not something an applicant can finish over a weekend. Before accepting an offer, ask: *Has the company agreed to start a permanent-residence case, and who is responsible for the first filing?* A recruiter can truthfully say the company sponsors work visas while meaning only a temporary H-1B. That answer may still be worth taking. It is not the same offer.

**4. Family sponsorship in a capped category.** A green-card holder can petition for a spouse; a U.S. citizen aged 21 or older can petition for a sibling. Neither relationship belongs to the uncapped group in first place. For a family petition, the *priority date* normally marks when the petition was filed, not when it was approved. Compare it with the *Final Action Dates* in the State Department’s monthly [Visa Bulletin](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-bulletin.html). The country counted for the numerical limit is usually your birthplace. Two Russian citizens sitting in the same consular waiting room can therefore belong to different queues.

Why put a job ahead of family? A newly filed sibling case can face a much longer statutory queue than an employer’s case. But if your sister filed years ago and a number is now available, I would put that established family case ahead of a new job offer. The date on one old receipt can change the ranking more than a promotion can.

## Fifth and sixth: capital and chance

**5. Qualifying investment through EB-5.** The investment capital must be lawfully obtained and placed at risk, and the investment must generally account for at least ten qualifying full-time U.S. jobs per investor. If the money comes from selling a flat in Russia, the sale price is only part of the story. Can you document how you acquired the asset and trace the proceeds? Will the institutions involved lawfully process that particular transfer? Find out before paying a nonrefundable deposit. This route ranks low because the downside is unusually concrete: you can lose money even if the immigration case succeeds.

**6. The Diversity Visa lottery.** Last as a plan does not mean last on your calendar. If you qualify and an official entry window opens, it can be sensible to enter while pursuing something else. Eligibility usually follows country of birth rather than citizenship. Selection gives you a chance to pursue a visa, not a reserved visa, and the program year has a deadline. I could not confirm the DV-2027 registration status on November 19, 2026; use the [State Department’s Diversity Visa instructions](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/immigrate/diversity-visa-program-entry.html). A page that lets past entrants check results is not evidence that new entries are open.

## When the ranking is the wrong tool

An intracompany transfer on L-1 status can be an excellent reason to move. Neither L-1 nor H-1B, however, matures into a green card after a certain number of paychecks. Rank the permanent petition the employer is prepared to pursue, not the flight it is prepared to buy.

An F-1 student visa has its own temporary-intent requirements; admission to a degree program does not put you in an immigrant queue.

If the real issue is fear of persecution for a legally protected reason, such as political opinion, this is a safety decision, not route seven. U.S. asylum is sought by people present in or arriving in the United States; refugee resettlement from abroad is a different process. Seek confidential, individual legal advice promptly rather than waiting for a lottery or a job offer.

For an ordinary planned move, start with two pieces of paper: the strongest evidence that you qualify, and the current official guidance on whether your case can proceed. If you have the first but not the second, you have a reason to prepare. You do not yet have a moving date.
