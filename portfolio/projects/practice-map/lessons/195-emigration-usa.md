# The Queue and the Gate: Ranking the Ways to Emigrate to the USA as a Russian Citizen, 2026

America has no form for “I would like to move, please.” It has doors, each cut for one kind of person. Ranked by a single measure — of the people who can honestly start down a route, how many end up with a green card, and how soon — the order for a Russian citizen in late 2026 is: close family of a US citizen, self-petitioned talent, investment, an employer, the student ladder, the lottery, and asylum. The order is steadier than the news; the news is dated below.

## Three locks on every door

Every route passes the same three locks. The **category** is the law’s description of who may come: a citizen’s wife, a physicist, an investor. Congress wrote the current menu in 1990 and has hardly touched it since, which is why presidents fight over everything else.

The **queue** is the annual cap. Most categories have a fixed number of green cards a year, and no country may take more than seven percent. Indians and Chinese therefore wait in long national lines; Russians stand in the general line, which for work-based green cards is the shortest there is. The queue goes by where you were born, not by your passport.

The **gate** is a person: a consular officer abroad, or an immigration officer if you are already lawfully in the US. The gate goes by passport and residence, and here the Russian passport is a burden. The embassy in Moscow has not run a normal visa section since 2021 (the State Department’s term for such applicants is “homeless”), so, as of November 2026, Russians interview in Warsaw for immigrant visas and in Warsaw or Astana for temporary ones, unless they legally live elsewhere. Astana needs no visa. Warsaw needs a Schengen visa, sparingly issued: a queue to join a queue. So: good in the queue, bad at the gate. For an Indian engineer it is the reverse: consulates at home, a queue of decades.

This is why much online advice, written for the largest groups of applicants, fits Russians badly. It says: climb a ladder of temporary visas. But a ladder is a series of gates, and on most temporary-visa forms wanting to stay in America is the wrong answer. On an immigrant petition it is the only acceptable one. For a Russian, routes with one gate beat routes with five.

## The ranking

Nobody publishes completion rates by route and nationality, so this is a judged order, not a statistic. It assumes you start from outside the US; time estimates are from 2025.

**1. Close family of a citizen.** The spouse, the unmarried child under 21, or the parent of an adult US citizen. No cap, no lottery, one real gate; usually one to two years. You cannot simply decide to qualify: a marriage made for the papers is a federal crime and a lifelong bar. More distant relatives (a citizen’s sibling, a green-card holder’s spouse) have categories too, with queues of years to decades.

**2. Talent you can document.** Two categories let you petition for yourself, without an employer: extraordinary ability (EB-1A) and the national-interest waiver (NIW). One officer’s judgement, then the short queue: roughly one to three years. NIW filings boomed after 2022 and approvals grew harder to win; treat any “guaranteed” package with the suspicion it has earned.

**3. Money.** The investor category (EB-5) asks for $800,000 in a rural or high-unemployment project, or $1,050,000 elsewhere, and ten jobs. It is arithmetic rather than opinion, and Russians face no national queue. But every dollar needs a documented lawful origin and a documented path out of Russia through unsanctioned banks. And the law has dates in it: by my reading, the amounts rise with inflation on 1 January 2027, the “regional center” programme most investors use is authorised only to 30 September 2027, and petitions filed after 30 September 2026 are unprotected if it lapses, unless Congress has acted since. The Gold Card (since December 2025: a $1 million gift plus a $15,000 fee) rides on the talent categories, so it buys eligibility, not a shorter queue, and a gift is not designed to come back. The small-business investor visa other nationalities use (E-2) does not exist for a Russian passport: no treaty.

**4. An employer.** Either transfer (a year at a multinational’s office outside the US, then an L-1 visa) or hire, through the H-1B, a March lottery that a December 2025 rule weights by salary level. Both permit the honest answer about staying. The weakness is the number of strangers who must say yes: the employer, the lottery, the Labor Department (whose test has lately taken about two years), then the gate.

**5. The student ladder.** Student visa, post-study work permit, H-1B lottery, labour-market test, green card: five gates, and the first is an officer in Astana or Warsaw who must believe you plan to go home afterwards. It is the dearest route per unit of certainty. Its one real asset: it puts you inside the US, where later steps can skip the consulate.

**6. The lottery.** Open, in recent years, to nearly anyone born in Russia with a completed secondary education. The odds (around one in a hundred a year or worse, from memory) round to no; the price rounds to nothing. Being drawn is not a visa: roughly twice as many people are drawn as there are visas, and unissued visas die on 30 September. As of mid-November 2026 registration is not open at all.

**7. Asylum.** Last, for a different reason: nobody can state its odds in 2026, and failing costs more than a refusal letter. It is protection, not a plan, and can be requested only on US soil or at the border; no embassy takes claims, and the parole programmes of recent years were never for Russians. The border app Russians used in 2023–24 closed in January 2025; a proclamation then shut the southern border to most claims, the immigration service froze decisions on its pending cases in December 2025, long detention became a real outcome, and all of it is in court. If you are in danger, this is not a ranking question; find a lawyer who does only this.

Not on the list: the tourist visa. It is not a way to emigrate, and entering on one while concealing a plan to stay can earn a permanent bar.

## What expires

What lasts: the categories, the birthplace queue, the split between forms that forbid the wish to stay and forms that require it, the 30 September lottery deadline, and the fact that asylum cannot be requested from abroad. The rest is weather.

I wrote from memory, without live sources; my own knowledge is firm to about January 2026, and anything later comes from a fact sheet gathered on 19 November 2026 that I could not check.

Russia is in no US entry ban (June 2025, widened to 39 countries in December 2025). On 21 January 2026 immigrant-visa issuance was paused for 75 nationalities, Russians included; the fact sheet says a court vacated the pause on 21 August 2026 and issuance resumed, with an appeal pending. It was a gate measure, by passport, at consulates — and a seven-month pause is exactly what a 30 September lottery deadline cannot survive; what became of 2026’s Russian winners I could not confirm.

The $100,000 charge on H-1B petitions for workers abroad (September 2025) is reported vacated and uncollected since late October 2026, with a similar fee proposed by regulation. Fees this year: $185 for most temporary-visa applications, a $250 “visa integrity fee” arriving unevenly, $1 to enter the lottery. Students and several other classes must make their social media public. And I could not confirm whether the immigration service’s separate holds on applications from “high-risk” countries ever covered Russians; my sources disagree.

The current answer lives in four places: the State Department’s monthly Visa Bulletin (queues), travel.state.gov (pauses and which consulate takes Russians), uscis.gov (fees and forms), and dvprogram.state.gov, the lottery’s only real address.

## What the order is saying

The ranking runs almost opposite to the width of the door. The surest routes admit almost nobody to the doorstep; the one route open to everybody is a drum of numbers. The system always rations somewhere: by who you are before you start, or by judgement, waiting and luck after. So the useful question is not which route is best but which doors you are actually standing at. Usually it is one or two. Work on those, count the gates, and when the lottery reopens, enter it as well. It is sixth by certainty and first by price.
