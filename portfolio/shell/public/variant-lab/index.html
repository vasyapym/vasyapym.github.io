Variant lab — scroll-driven presentations

One shared scroll engine, five structurally different presentations. Each keeps the card component untouched and holds the contract: one pinned card, one illustration in view, native document scroll, no snap, no scroll-trap. Cards are fake static stand-ins; judge the motion. Desktop ≥ 1024px only — phones get a plain stack; reduced-motion gets hard cuts.

S1 Plotter S2 Cut S3 Approach S4 Lens S5 Margin notes

S1Plotter — draw-off / draw-on

One fixed drafting sheet. Nothing moves; the ink does. The current illustration is erased stroke by stroke while the next is plotted in, like a pen plotter redrawing the sheet. Rail caret tracks progress.

S2Cut — hairline guillotine wipe

A single frame; between cards a 1px rule descends (then ascends, alternating) across the sheet. Above the rule the next card, below it the current one. The rule is the transition — no fade. Cheapest of the five: pure clip-path.

S3Approach — z-travel through frames

A corridor of framed drawings; scroll moves you forward. The current frame grows past you and leaves; the next approaches as a hairline rectangle far ahead, becoming real only once it is close. Thin ink gets thinner with distance — on brand.

S4Lens — zoom into a detail, out into the next

The camera pushes into a chosen detail of the current drawing until the lines dissolve into a fine grid, then pulls back out — and the drawing that materialises is the next project. Continuity through a shared abstract state, not a cut.

S5Margin notes — pinned illustration, free-scrolling text

The only variant where the text is not pinned: the right column is six text blocks at reading speed, the left plate shows whichever block is nearest the centre, with a fast stroke draw-on. Not a slideshow — an article whose figure keeps up.
